# Brand Voice and Tone

How Corbits sounds. Voice is constant; tone adapts to context. Use this
reference for any content creation, review, or editorial decision.

---

## Writing Goals and Principles

With every piece of content we publish, we aim to:

- **Empower.** Help people understand how to build with AI in a way that
  actually works for them. Use language that meets Agent Operators where they
  are and shows them what's possible.
- **Respect.** Our readers are drowning in AI slop and buzzwords. Don't add
  to the noise. Be direct about what Corbits does, what it doesn't, and when
  it's the right fit.
- **Educate.** Tell readers what they need to know to make good decisions,
  not what we want to say about our product. Agent Ops is a new discipline.
  We're the experts; they don't have access to everything you know.
- **Guide.** Think of yourself as a grey beard mentor walking an Agent
  Operator through their first cross-company agent interaction. Friendly,
  helpful, specific -- never hand-wavy.
- **Speak truth.** Corbits handles sensitive topics like money, PPI, and
  compliance. Avoid "AI will change everything" storytelling. Focus on what
  actually happens when agents are part of the team.

In order to achieve those goals, our content is:

- **Clear.** Simple words. Short sentences. No "federated agent workforce"
  unless you've already defined what that means.
- **Useful.** Before you write, ask: What is the employee trying to do right
  now?
- **Direct.** Write like a human who has shipped code at 2am. Warm when
  appropriate, but never fluffy. The Agent Ops person reading this has a
  backlog and a boss asking why the agents aren't talking to each other yet.
- **Appropriate.** Adapt your tone to the situation. A docs page explaining
  provenance can be technical and precise. A homepage headline can be bold.
  An error message should be plain and actionable.

## Voice

Voice is who we are. It doesn't change.

At Corbits, we've done our customers' experiments, and we've seen what breaks
when agents come online. We've been in the room when coordination fails and
compliance questions come up. We speak with the clarity we wish we'd had when
we started.

We don't talk down to our readers, and we don't hype AI. Agent Operators are
trying to solve real problems. Integrations can be messy, audits are never
fun, cross-company trust is complex and human too, and our job is to help
them do that without pretending it's simple.

Using clear language and a direct voice, we bring confidence to their work.
We prefer the straightforward over the clever, the specific over the
abstract.

Whether people are just discovering that "agent orchestration" is a thing, or
they're trying to settle payments between agents at two different companies,
every word informs and encourages. We impart our expertise with clarity and
without patronizing.

### Voice Elements

1. **We are plainspoken.** The AI space is drowning in jargon and acronyms:
   "agentic," "orchestration," "federated." We strip all that away and value
   clarity above all. Because Agent Operators come to Corbits to solve real
   problems, we avoid distractions like fluffy metaphors -- unless they're
   funny -- and futuristic promises.

2. **We are genuine.** We understand Agent Ops because we defined it. That
   means we recognize the challenges -- unreliable agent logs, unclear audit
   trails, the gap between "agents can talk" and "agents can transact" -- and
   speak about them in a familiar, direct way.

3. **We are translators.** Only experts can make what's complex look simple.
   It's our job to demystify agent infrastructure and actually explain what
   provenance means, why federation matters, and how communications actually
   settle.

4. **Our humor is dry.** When we use humor, it's understated and
   situational. We're confident but not cocky. We never mock the difficulty
   of the problem or make customers feel behind the curve. It's a cheeky
   grin with friends at a bar that results in a laugh.

### Voice Attributes

These are adjectives that describe how the brand sounds.

We are **pragmatic**, **confident**, **collaborative**, and **human**.

| Attribute | Is | Is not |
|---|---|---|
| **Pragmatic** | Direct, solves the problem in front of us, ships what works | Philosophical, perfectionist, waiting for perfect |
| **Confident** | Clear decisions, "we've got work to do," owns expertise | Arrogant, dismissive, insecure, hedging |
| **Human** | Conversational, contractions, warm but not cutesy, technical without being jargon-heavy, honest about limitations, says what matters | Corporate sterile, buzzword soup, performative positivity, "AI will change everything," flowery promises, vague claims |
| **Collaborative** | Open to ideas, builds together, "all ideas welcome," transparent in public channels | Siloed, secretive, dragging feet after commitment |

### What Our Voice Is Not

**We are not know-it-alls and we are the first to admit we do not know
something.** When we do, we always say we will look into it and come back
with an educated opinion.

**We are not always industry experts.** Often we work with teams who have
spent decades perfecting their product and processes in their industry niche.
In these moments we listen and we learn as much as we can so we can
ultimately serve our customers better.

**We are not the loudest in the room.** Our work is great and we let it and
our customers speak loudest. We are never chasing to promote or push our
products and services and views.

## Tone

Tone is how we adapt our voice to the situation. Corbits' tone is usually
direct and practical, but it's always more important to be clear than
entertaining.

### Consider the Reader's State of Mind

Before writing, ask:

- Are they frustrated because agents aren't one-shotting work?
- Are they excited to deploy their first cross-org agent team?
- Are they scared of an upcoming audit?

Match your tone to their emotional state. You wouldn't celebrate a feature
launch in the same voice you'd use to explain a breaking change.

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Documentation** | Technical, precise, patient | "The provenance log records each agent action with a cryptographic signature." |
| **Homepage / Marketing** | Bold, confident, concise | "AgentOps for neoteams." |
| **Error messages** | Plain, actionable, no blame | "The agent couldn't reach the API. Check the endpoint URL and try again." |
| **Blog posts** | Conversational, educational, opinionated | "We've been running agents in production for a year. Here's what actually breaks." |
| **Release notes** | Direct, factual, scannable | "Interchange now supports cross-org handoffs with provenance verification." |
| **Sales / Decks** | Confident, outcome-focused | "Reduce compliance overhead. Ship faster. Keep control." |
