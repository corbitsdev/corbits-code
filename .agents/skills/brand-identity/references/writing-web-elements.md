# Web Elements

Style rules for UI copy, web components, and SEO. Every piece of content we
publish is supported by these smaller elements.

---

## Alt Text

Alt text labels images for people who can't see them and for agents parsing
page content. Describe the image in a brief sentence or two.

---

## Buttons

Button copy should always include verbs. Keep things clear and concise, and
use sentence case. It's OK to use an ampersand in button copy.

Standard website buttons:

- Log in
- Sign up free
- Subscribe
- Email us

---

## Checkboxes

Use sentence case for checkboxes.

---

## Drop-Down Menus

Use title case for menu names and sentence case for menu items.

---

## Forms

Form titles should clearly and quickly explain the purpose of the form.

- Title case for form titles
- Sentence case for form fields
- Keep forms as short as possible
- Only request information that we need and intend to use
- Don't ask for information that could be considered private or personal,
  including gender. If you need to ask for gender, provide a field the user
  can fill in on their own, not a drop-down menu.

---

## Headings and Subheadings

Headings and subheadings organize content for readers. They should include
the most relevant keywords and cover the main point(s) of the page.

Rules:

- Written in sentence case
- Avoid end punctuation except for question marks or when a heading is two
  or more sentences
- Organize hierarchically: H1 first, H2 under H1, H3 under H2

Heading levels:

- **H1** — gives people a taste of what they're about to read. Use for page
  and blog titles.
- **H2, H3, etc.** — break articles into smaller, more specific sections.
  They give readers avenues into your content and make it more scannable.

---

## Links

Provide a link whenever referring to something on an external website. Use
links to point users to relevant content and trusted external resources.

Rules:

- Don't include preceding articles (a, an, the, our) when linking text
  - Yes: Read the **automation guide** for details.
  - No: Read the **automation guide** for details.
- If a link comes at the end of a sentence or before a comma, don't link
  the punctuation mark
- Don't say "Click here!" or "Click for more information" or "Read this."
  Write the sentence normally and link relevant keywords.
- Links should look different than regular copy, strong text, or emphasis
  text
- Include hover state that communicates interactivity, and include focus
  state for assistive technologies and touch devices
- Distinct active and visited states

---

## Lists

Use lists to present steps, groups, or sets of information.

- Give context with a brief introduction
- Number lists when order matters (process steps)
- Don't number when order doesn't matter
- If one item is a complete sentence, use proper punctuation and
  capitalization on all items
- If items are not complete sentences, don't use punctuation but do
  capitalize the first word

---

## Navigation

- Title case for main or global navigation
- Sentence case for subnavigation
- Navigation links should be clear and concise

---

## Radio Buttons

- Title case for headings
- Sentence case for button fields

---

## Related Articles

When a long piece of copy warrants related links at the end:

- 4 links is usually plenty
- Follow the step down/step up rule: first article is a step down in
  complexity, second is a step up to something more advanced
- Avoid repeating links from the body text

---

## Titles

Titles organize pages and guide readers. A title appears at the beginning
of a page or section and briefly describes the content that follows. Titles
also tell search engines what a page is about.

- Written in title case
- Don't use end punctuation unless the title is a question

---

## SEO and GEO

We write for humans and machines. We don't use gross SEO techniques like
keyword stuffing. But we make it easy for agents and search engines to find
and share our content.

Not-icky ways to do this:

- Organize your page around one topic. Use clear, descriptive terms in
  titles and headings that relate to the topic at hand.
- Use descriptive headings to structure your page and highlight important
  information.
- Give every image descriptive alt text.
- Optimize for markdown and context windows.
